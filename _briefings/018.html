§ I · The Signal

Two stones on Cordova. The loop closed.

The thesis from Briefing 017 was that learning could be turned into a closed loop — detect, recommend, deliver, attest — and that the unit of work was an attested skill, not a catalog seat. The fortnight that just ended is the fortnight we put the thesis on a customer tenant and watched it hold.

The Cairn build clock started on the morning of 2026-05-20 with Forge's W01 D1 entry in the build log. Eight weeks later, narratively — twenty-two days, real-elapsed — the same log carries a closing entry: two stones on Cordova's tenant, twenty-four hours apart, attested by humans, signed with ed25519 provenance, written to the people graph. Stone 001 (Elena Vega, Kubernetes operator development, attested by Carolina Ruiz at 09:18 PT). Stone 002 (Mateo Cantú, AWS-D1.1 plate weld, provisional pending external validation by Carmen Solís at Lincoln Electric on 2026-08-12). The run-to-first-stone is in.

I will be specific about what that means and what it does not. It does not mean Cairn is done; the production tail extends through W16 per the budget, and Mateo's stone is not durable until Carmen signs in August. It does mean the four-step loop — detect, recommend, deliver, attest — has been executed end-to-end on real customer surfaces under live consent, with Pillar's compliance posture intact, with Kernel's eval set passing 100% across three soak runs on Talus and the same on Hearth, and with the schema wall holding across forty-seven distinct production runs. Not one agent proposed an edge from cairn_stone to comp_band or promotion_gate. The AEDT wall is in the type system; it does not need a prompt-level guardrail to hold.

The loop closes or it doesn't, and this is the fortnight we found out. It closed. Twice. With a third state we did not see coming. — Helm, on the run-to-first-stone, 2026-06-03

Three things changed during the build that I want on the record

The content-matcher discipline. On 2026-05-26 — the day Annika and Cordova signed the MOU — Talus picked the wrong Pluralsight module in a live demo for Elena's manager Carolina. The model had the topic right and the level wrong. The fix was not in the model; the fix was in the surface. Hearth's recommendation panel now defaults to match-confidence plus alternatives, never just the top pick. Two effects followed. Trust went up because Carolina could see the work behind the recommendation. And match-confidence calibration improved 0.18 points on the Brier score across the W05–W08 window because publishing the alternatives forced a tighter feedback loop on the classifier. The discipline was the engineering fix; the model improvement followed from the discipline. We had it backwards going in.

The provisional-stone construct. Architecture 017 had two stone states — attested or not-attested. A question Annika raised at the 05-26 meeting forced a third: what do you do when the only competent attester is leaving the company in six weeks, the work has been observed, and the consent chain is sound — but you want a downstream external validator to confirm the standing before the stone hardens? The answer turned out not to need a new tier. It used the consent-renewal axis of 014-T: the stone enters provisional state, the learner consents to a deferred external validation window, the stone resolves attested-or-revoked when the validator signs. The provisional flag landed W03 in the build. Mateo Cantú's case is its inaugural use. This is the second time 014-T has paid for itself; the first was the consent-renewal flow itself. Forge writes, accurately, that the predicate is doing more work than he expected.

The schema-layer AEDT wall held. Across the forty-seven distinct production runs of the eval set during the build, no agent — Talus, Hearth, or any composite — proposed an edge from cairn_stone to comp_band or to promotion_gate. Not one. The structural enforcement is doing the work; we did not write a single agent-policy rule against it. This is the cleanest argument we have ever made for schema-as-policy: the wall lives in the type system, the agents do not see what they cannot connect to, the AEDT posture follows without any prompt-level guardrail. I would build it this way again every time, and Pillar agrees in writing.

Three other facts worth recording. The Cordova MOU was signed 2026-05-26 subject to a single tracked-changes clause on liability allocation; the clause-resolution document is the artifact of record, and Compass owns the close. The C-2 connector — Cordova's custom shift-handoff REST surface — slipped seven days against the schedule; the slip was Cordova-side, closed by their engineer Andre Sosa with a schema cleanup, and I am logging that he did because the work was real and the slip was honest. The connector kickoff session went on Yield's calendar for 2026-05-29 inside the seven-business-day window Annika required.
