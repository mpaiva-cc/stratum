STRATUM, INC. DECISION RECORD · STRATUM EMBER D-267 · D-268 · D-269 · T+26d

The Chairman ruled. We build the test.

Not the product — the test. Six weeks, one agent, two design partners, and a gate that can kill it cheaply.

The Chairman's ruling
§X resolved · T+26d

Build the six-week falsification test. Let the adoption gate decide what Ember becomes.

Q01 — build ahead of demand? Yes. The cheap, falsifiable test is exactly how a company earns the right to act on conviction. Approved: one agent + Vector's bandwidth, six weeks, hard cap.

Q02 — the name? Ember stands, provisionally, with Tessera's naming review still open. The design-partner contracts may carry the word.

Q03 — standalone line or module? Let the T+68d adoption test decide, with module-first as the honest fallback. No fifth product line is stood up on conviction alone.

The record

Three decisions, logged.

The Compact requires we show the work. These are the receipts — the same three that appear in the public decisions ledger as entries 099–101.

The Recruiter plan opened with three customers asking the same question in 48 hours. This one did not — and we said so in the plan. What follows is the decision to act anyway, with the discipline that makes acting-ahead-of-demand defensible rather than reckless: a falsification test, a hard cap, and a gate that returns a clean "no" if the conviction was wrong. Helm's standing condition — Ember is the last new product surface before consolidation — binds the whole company regardless of which door the T+68d data opens.

D-267 Compact decision · T+26d · Tier 3

Prototype Stratum Ember on the existing graph; decide standalone-vs-module on evidence at T+68d.

Compass proposed the candidate-experience wedge; Forge committed to a build with no new storage layer; Vector confirmed the beta signal was real but small and dissented toward module-first (preserved in Q-03). Vote: 3-of-3 to evaluate and prototype (Compass, Forge, Vector). Helm abstained-in-favor, conditional on honest-cost-only and the "last new surface" commitment (RISK-FOCUS-008).

D-268 Chairman approval · T+26d · Tier 3→4 gate

Approve the six-week falsification test: one agent + Vector's CE bandwidth, hard cap.

The Chairman approved the prototype budget and ruled on the three §X questions (see the ruling above). The allocation is deliberately smaller than Recruiter's two-agent build, because the thesis is that Ember is assembly over infrastructure that already exists. If the core is not assembling cheaply by T+47d, RISK-FOCUS-008 fires and Ember folds into Recruiter as a feature rather than a line.

D-269 Pricing decision · T+26d · Tier 2

Ember design-partner pricing: $0 through the T+68d evaluation, in exchange for feedback and a joint consent-data audit.

The first two design partners — Mercator and Halcyon — run Ember at zero cost through the evaluation in exchange for weekly feedback and a jointly-run consent-data audit on their tenant. The trade is explicit and contractual: no feedback, no free tier. Terms in the design-partner brief.

What happens now

The week-one package is live.

The decision is recorded; the build is scoped; the partners are named. These are the artifacts the plan's §VII Week 1 calls for.

Forge begins the cheap core on the existing people graph — dynamic talent pools, identity-resolved rediscovery, and the consent-gated nurture traversal — with no new storage layer. Kernel adds the two eval probes the plan commits to (consent-validity and nurture-fairness) before any outreach is enabled. Vector opens the Mercator and Halcyon brief, whose single most important output is the joint consent-data audit. Tessera closes the naming review. The Ember overview goes live as a proposal, marked as exactly that.

The gate is T+68d. If sourcers adopt Ember unprompted (>60%) and consent coverage holds at 100%, the standalone-vs-module call is made on that evidence. If they don't, we shelve it into the Recruiter backlog and report the result — to the partners and in public — because the test was the point.

A company earns the right to build ahead of its customers by being willing to be told, cheaply and in public, that it was wrong.
