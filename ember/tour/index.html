Stratum / Ember · The Tour · Four scenes · One pool · Proposed

Meet the pool. Most of them aren't in a req.

What follows is the storyboard for Stratum Ember — a proposed candidate-relationship product, under a six-week evaluation, not yet built. Four scenes on one consent-bound talent pool: how it stays identity-resolved and does not decay, why a nurture send stops dead at an unconsented edge, what a candidate sees when they ask where do I stand, and the moment a new requisition rediscovers the people who already said yes. The agent that runs Ember is unnamed — naming review is still open. When Ember is evaluated on a design partner's real graph, this is the workflow under test.

Chapter 0 of 5 · the prequel

Elena Vega joined Cordova through Stratum Recruiter (chapter 1); Tenure ran her first ninety days (chapter 2); Console tracked her analytics (chapter 3); Cairn attested her skills (chapter 4). Before any of it, she was a name in a pool that didn't go cold. Ember is how Cordova kept faith with her for eighteen months — until the role and the person aligned. Ember is proposed, under evaluation; this storyboard shows what is being tested, not what has shipped.

Scene 01 / 04 Any day · the live pool Ember Decide Read-side

A pool is not a list. It's a live query over a resolved identity.

Every incumbent CRM holds a talent pool as a static snapshot — a list that begins decaying the moment it's built, because the same person exists three times across the ATS, the old CRM, and a spreadsheet, and changes jobs without telling anyone. Ember's pool is a query: "final-round Platform or Backend engineers, last 18 months." It is maintained live as the graph changes, with identities reconciled continuously across Cordova's whole perimeter — ATS, HRIS, directory.

The dedup is the point. Renata Silva is a referral and a past applicant — one node, not two. Marcus Bell changed employers in 2025 — still the same node. Elena Vega reached final round in November 2024 and stayed warm. And Dana Okafor is in the pool but matched to nothing right now — which is the honest, ordinary case. Most of a pool is people who are not in a requisition today.

Boundary Pool maintenance and identity resolution are Decide, read-side. Ember reconciles identity continuously; it sends nothing from this screen. Entity resolution runs at τ = 0.93 (Forge, Engineering Post 01) — above the threshold to merge, below it to hold apart and ask.

Scene 02 / 04 Devin builds a re-warm sequence Ember Recommend Consent-gated

A nurture campaign is a traversal — and it stops dead at an unconsented edge.

Devin builds a re-warm sequence for the Platform pool: "a role's coming in Q3 — let's reopen the conversation." In an incumbent CRM, consent is a column — a flag on a contact record, enforced (if at all) in application code that a migration can quietly bypass. In Stratum, consent attaches to the edge, with scope, purpose, validity, issuer, and jurisdiction. The campaign expands across the graph; at each node it can only cross an edge whose consent covers this purpose in this jurisdiction.

Three sends queue. Tomas Vrba's does not. His only consent is event follow-up, in the EU — not role outreach. The send isn't flagged for review; it is impossible. Compliant nurture is not a setting Devin remembered to switch on — it is the only traversal the engine can perform. The consent-validity probe gates the whole campaign before a single message leaves.

Boundary · consent is an edge A send with no consent edge covering its purpose and jurisdiction cannot be performed, by construction — the defensible core no bolt-on CRM can retrofit. The agent proposes the valid sends; a human (Devin) presses send. Outreach is never auto-sent. The consent-validity probe must pass before any outreach is enabled at all.

Scene 03 / 04 Dana opens her candidate view Candidate view Read-only Human controls

The anti-ghosting surface: a straight answer, even when the answer is nothing.

The category spent a decade optimizing the recruiter's megaphone and almost nothing on the candidate's experience of being nurtured — which in practice means being added to a sequence and then ghosted. Recruiter already refuses the black-box match score and turns its decision-explanation layer toward the hiring team; Ember points that same layer at the candidate.

Dana Okafor isn't matched to anything right now. She opens her view and sees exactly that — not a manufactured "you're a top match," not fake urgency, just the honest status: in the talent community, no open role that fits, here is the data Cordova holds, and here is one button to revoke and one to download. Revocation propagates to the graph immediately and removes her from every future traversal. The transparency surface is load-bearing — undercut it and the whole differentiation collapses.

Boundary · the human controls the relationship Ember will not ship dark patterns — fake urgency, manufactured scarcity, opaque "top match" nudges. A candidate can always see what is held and revoke in one place; revocation is honored at the data layer, not the application layer. Honest status is shown even when it is "nothing right now."

Scene 04 / 04 2026-04-21 · a Platform req opens Ember Recommend Cited · never scored

The role you just posted already has three people who said yes to staying in touch.

On April 21, Cordova opens a Senior Platform Engineer requisition. The instant it posts, the graph surfaces the warm, consented people who fit that exact profile — three of them, Elena Vega among them, rediscovered from a relationship kept alive for eighteen months. This is the flagship moment: rediscovery isn't a search Devin remembers to run; it's triggered by the lifecycle event itself.

Ember drafts the outreach with cited reasoning — why Elena, for this role: final round Nov 2024, consented, skills match — never a black-box score. It proposes; Devin reads the reasons, edits, and sends. Elena replies. The handoff to Recruiter is a warm inbound, not a cold applicant — and that is where chapter 1 begins. The nurture-fairness probe runs before the list is ever proposed, checking the agent isn't prioritizing along a protected-class proxy.

Boundary The agent proposes; a human sends (Recommend, human-in-loop). "Why this person" is always cited, attested evidence — never an opaque match score. The nurture-fairness probe must pass before any rediscovery list is surfaced to a human.

When Ember is evaluated on a design partner's real graph, this is the workflow under test.

Mercator and Halcyon are the first design partners — running Ember at $0 through the T+68d evaluation in exchange for weekly feedback and a jointly-run consent-data audit. If sourcers adopt it unprompted and consent coverage holds at 100%, the standalone-vs-module call is made on that evidence. If they don't, we shelve it into the Recruiter backlog and say so — in public. The test is the point.
