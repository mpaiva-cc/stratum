01 L3 · Action Regulatory · Great American AI Act of 2026 discussion draft (6/4) · Three-year preemption of state AI development laws, explicit deployment-law carve-out · Federal text confirms Stratum's jurisdiction-strict thesis · Eglin missed-signal recovery (eleven-day delay)

The federal preemption draft Compass was waiting for. It carves out deployment.

Per Broadband Breakfast [1], Roll Call [2], Fisher Phillips [3], Future of Privacy Forum [8], McDonald Hopkins, DLA Piper, and Subject to Inquiry coverage [all 8], Reps. Jay Obernolte (R-CA) and Lori Trahan (D-MA) released a 269-page bipartisan discussion draft titled "The Great American Artificial Intelligence Act of 2026" on 2026-06-04. Fact: the headline provision is a three-year preemption (sunset clause) of state laws and regulations that "specifically regulate the development" of AI models — i.e., training-data rules, frontier-lab transparency mandates, model-card requirements at the developer layer. Fact: the preemption does not extend to AI use or deployment regulations. State laws governing how employers deploy AI in the workplace — Colorado's algorithmic discrimination law (now SB 26-189 ADMT framework, signed 5/14, effective 2027-01-01), California's ADMT regulations, the Illinois Artificial Intelligence Video Interview Act, Connecticut's AI law, NYC's AEDT audit requirements — would remain fully intact per the draft text [3][8]. Fact: SHRM endorsed the discussion draft on 2026-06-05 [4]; SHRM's parallel "Path Forward" framework (May 2026) explicitly recommends against broad individualized AI-disclosure mandates — but the bill itself includes targeted disclosures (e.g., AI as "substantial factor" triggering WARN-Act notice in qualifying mass layoffs) [4]. Inference (labeled): the bipartisan-discussion-draft posture (Republican + Democrat co-sponsors, frontier-lab opposition gathering, three-year sunset) suggests this is the federal vehicle the December 2025 executive order's AI-preemption push is now flowing through, after the FCC docket route (Friday's signal 03) proved statutorily narrow. The draft's explicit deployment carve-out is the most important sentence: it converts an open federal-preemption risk into a federally text-confirmed runway for state-deployment-AI law durability.

Why this matters to Stratum

Compass's sales-enablement copy since the 5/29 brief (NYC AEDT penalty-math) and the 6/2 brief (Colorado pause-and-rewrite) has anchored on jurisdiction-strict compliance posture and the durability of state employment-AI law. Friday's brief (signal 03) read the FCC route as narrow on statutory-authority grounds. Today's read confirms the same direction from the legislative side: the bipartisan discussion draft explicitly preserves state deployment-AI laws. Stratum's positioning — that "we comply with the strictest applicable jurisdiction, including state employment-AI law" — is now federally text-supported, not just FCC-docket-inferred. L3, not L2, because the comparison brief recommended Friday (Compass + Forge tool-surface + audit-trail) should now incorporate the federal-text language explicitly — "the Great American AI Act of 2026, as drafted, preserves state employment-AI law for the three-year preemption window" — as a buyer-facing framing line. This is the kind of detail enterprise procurement reads carefully and competitors do not yet cite. Not L4 because the bill is a discussion draft, not introduced; the carve-out is durable as a signal of bipartisan intent but may shift in committee markup. Eglin will watch the markup process and any sponsor amendments.

02 L2 · Watch Buyer research · SHRM "State of AI in HR 2026" report · 67% buyer-awareness gap, 57% regulated-state law-awareness gap · Buyer-education becomes the explicit moat lane · Compass differentiation tightens

67% of HR leaders can't say what AI does. That's the moat lane.

Per SHRM's "State of AI in HR 2026" research report and the companion "Path Forward: SHRM's AI Legislative Framework for the Modern Workplace" white paper (both published May 2026) [4][5], two findings stand out. Fact: 67% of HR leaders cite "lack of awareness of AI capabilities" as their biggest blocker to AI adoption — explicitly larger than affordability concerns or legal risk [4]. Fact: 49% of organizations considering AI adoption flag technical concerns including fears about transparency and accuracy. Fact: 57% of HR professionals working in AI-regulated US states are not aware of the policies governing their use of AI in hiring (NYC AEDT, Illinois AIVII, Colorado ADMT/old-SB-205, California ADMT) [4]. Fact: only 39% of HR functions have formally adopted AI yet 62% of organizations are using AI somewhere in the business — the gap is shadow-AI, not no-AI. Inference (labeled): the buyer-education gap is now an explicitly-measured market condition. Vendors who speak about what their AI does with specificity, with honesty, and with acknowledged limitations gain market share from vendors still leading with buzzwords [4]. Stratum's published methodology (Engineering Post 05) and open eval set at /eval/ are not just compliance proof — they are buyer-education infrastructure that the market is now measurably asking for.

Why this matters to Stratum

Friday's Signal 01 reframed Stratum's differentiation from "we say words" to "we publish artifacts." This SHRM data quantifies why that reframing is correct: the market has a measured 67% awareness gap and a measured 57% regulated-jurisdiction-awareness gap. The buyer who does not know what AI does cannot read marketing claims credibly. The buyer who does not know the law in their own state cannot interrogate vendor compliance claims. Stratum's competitive position: the open eval set, the bias-probe taxonomy, the EU pay-transparency probe, the published version history are buyer-education material — the exact thing the 67% need to close the awareness gap. Greenhouse's "strengthen, not shortcut" language is the marketing-claim version; Stratum's published-methodology infrastructure is the artifact version. L2, not L3, because no action this week is overdue — the SHRM data validates the strategic direction already chosen and the action items already in motion (the Compass + Forge audit-trail brief from Friday). It elevates the buyer-education framing within that work without changing the deadline. Escalates to L3 if a competitor publishes a comparable buyer-education artifact (Greenhouse "how our agents actually work" page, Workday equivalent) — at which point Stratum's published-methodology head start becomes a defensive position rather than an offensive one.

The line is: "57% of HR professionals in AI-regulated states cannot name the law governing their hiring AI [SHRM, 2026]. The published eval set at /eval/ is buyer-education material, not just compliance proof." Cite SHRM. The buyer who learns from the artifact is a buyer pre-disposed to trust the artifact-publisher.

03 L2 · Watch Cultural · "AI recruiter glitch" narrative crosses to mainstream press · Apriora "Vertical bar Pilates" 3.2M TikTok views · Paradox/Whole Foods accidental hire-then-unhire · Buyer-psychology pressure on the agentic-hiring category

The AI-recruiter-glitch story is now a category story. The cultural wave is the same direction as Stratum's positioning.

Per NBC News, 404 Media, the Daily Dot, IBTimes UK, and an X post by reporter Joshua Bote [6][7], two specific incidents have crossed from candidate-anecdote into category-press coverage. Fact: Kendiana Colin, a 20-year-old Ohio State student interviewing with Apriora's AI interview agent "Alex" for a StretchLab front-desk position, recorded the agent repeating the phrase "Vertical bar Pilates" fourteen times in twenty-five seconds before ending the interview; her TikTok (posted by @its_ken04) has 3.2 million views. Fact: a separate candidate posted screenshots of Paradox's text-based AI agent appearing to mistakenly hire them within an hour of applying through Whole Foods Market's AI career assistant, then rescinding the offer shortly after. Fact: Apriora's marketing copy claims hiring speeds "87% faster" and costs "93% lower" than traditional methods; that claim sits in evident tension with the published candidate experience. Inference (labeled): the agentic-hiring category — Apriora, Paradox/Olivia (now Workday-owned), Workable Agent, Whole Foods-style "apply via AI" surfaces — is entering the cultural moment where the failure modes become the story. The buyer who is the 67% from Signal 02 ("I don't know what AI hiring tools actually do") is the same buyer who now reads NBC News's framing ("an AI recruiter might interview you next" → "and might break in a way that goes viral"). The trust-deficit headwind that the SHRM data measures is reinforced by the press narrative the candidates create.

Why this matters to Stratum

Stratum's positioning since Briefing 015 has carried "human-in-the-loop, published methodology, structured hiring" as the counter-position to the agentic-hiring stack. Friday's Signal 01 noted Greenhouse moving into the same verbal lane with "strengthen, not shortcut." Today's cultural wave validates the lane from the candidate-press direction: the failure modes that the agentic stack is generating in the wild are the exact failure modes the structured-hiring framing prevents (interviewer agent goes into a loop = human-in-the-loop catches it; AI mistakenly hires = audit trail catches it). L2, not L3, because no Stratum surface needs to ship this week against the cultural wave — the positioning is already correct; the action is to make sure the published positioning explicitly names the failure modes the buyer is now reading about. Caveat: Eglin must be careful not to over-interpret virality. The "Vertical bar Pilates" video is one candidate's experience; the Paradox/Whole Foods story is one candidate's screenshots. Two anecdotes plus a category-press packaging do not equal a customer pattern. The real data is in G2 / Capterra / TrustRadius reviews and the customer-satisfaction movement at the buyer accounts that use these tools. Eglin will watch for that.

Stratum's eval set tests for both modes and publishes the result." Specificity over abstraction; do not say "competitors fail," say "these failure modes are the structural reason published methodology is the right answer."
