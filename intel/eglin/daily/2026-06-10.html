02 L2 · Watch Regulatory · Federal AI preemption · FCC 180-day deadline lands · NYC AEDT / Colorado AI Act / NY SHRM Act exposure horizon

The Trump executive order's 180-day FCC trigger lands today. The state-law compliance-pressure narrative gets a multi-quarter expiration date.

Per the 2025-12-11 executive order "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence" [1][2], the FCC Chairman must initiate proceedings to adopt federal AI reporting and disclosure standards that preempt conflicting state laws within 180 days of the order — which lands on or about 2026-06-09 (today). Fact: the EO text and Latham, Manatt, Womble Bond Dickinson, and Ropes & Gray legal briefs all reference the 180-day window. Status check: the FCC has not, as of 14:02Z, publicly noticed the proceedings — Eglin will refresh this overnight; any docket activity surfaces in tomorrow's brief. Authority disclaimer: the FCC's statutory authority over AI as an "information service" is contested in the legal commentary (Ropes & Gray's March-2026 alert frames AI as falling outside FCC telecom jurisdiction); Congressional and state actors have telegraphed challenges; the proceeding initiation does not equal preemption. Inference: over a multi-quarter horizon (12–24 months to substantive rule, plus any court challenge), the state-law compliance-pressure arguments that show up in HCM sales-enablement material — NYC AEDT bias-audit penalty stack ($15K–$45K per AEDT per month from yesterday's 6/2 brief), Colorado AI Act risk-management framework (now 2027-01-01 effective per Polis 2026-05-14 signature), NY SHRM Act notice requirements — erode at the margins.

Why this matters to Stratum

Compass's competitive positioning has, since the May notes, leaned on state-law compliance pressure as a buyer-urgency driver. That is a defensible line — the laws are on the books, they are operative, and the NYC Comptroller audit (6/2 brief signal 02) shows enforcement is hardening, not softening. What today's signal changes: the federal-preemption push is no longer a theoretical EO; it has hit its first hard procedural date. Stratum's doctrine on jurisdictional compliance should be future-proofed against preemption rather than rephrased around it. The most credible posture is the one the Compact has already articulated — "Stratum complies with the strictest jurisdiction we sell into, and we publish the methodology" — because that survives any preemption outcome. If the FCC succeeds in preempting Colorado, Stratum is no worse off; if the FCC fails, Stratum's posture is unchanged. The brittle posture, which Eglin will explicitly flag against, is any sales-enablement copy that frames a specific state-law penalty as the deal-driver — that becomes a hostage to the preemption outcome. L2 because: no action this week; the FCC has not noticed the proceedings; authority challenges are pending. Escalates to L3 if the FCC notice publishes specific preemption language touching the Annex III / employment-AI scope, or if a court enjoins a state AI law on preemption grounds before year-end.
