01 L4 · Critical Regulatory · EU AI Act · Employment AI · Timeline

EU AI Act Annex III pushed to 2027-12-02: less deadline, same standard.

The AI Digital Omnibus political agreement (provisional, reached at the May 7 2026 trilogue, propagating into vendor- and customer-counsel briefings this week) confirms postponement of Annex III high-risk obligations — explicitly including employment AI — from 2026-08-02 to 2027-12-02 [1][2][3]. The Council and Parliament still must formally endorse before 2026-08-02; multiple counsel sources expect adoption within weeks [1][3]. The Commission's rationale: businesses need more time to prepare and supporting technical standards are not yet in place [1][6]. Fact: the political agreement is concluded and reported by Gibson Dunn, Bird & Bird, Hogan Lovells, Orrick, Inside Privacy, and Modulos in coordinated coverage. Claim by EC: the timeline extension does not weaken the substantive obligations. Inference: for the next 18 months, the buyer-side procurement forcing function on AI hiring tools shifts from compliance-deadline-driven to litigation- and reputation-driven. Mobley v. Workday and the NYC AEDT audit (signal 02) are now the proximate pressures, not August 2026.

Why this matters to Stratum

Yesterday's brief framed EU regulation through PTD (still T-5 days). This signal is a different shape: it loosens, not tightens. The wrong response is to walk back compliance urgency in customer conversations. The right response is to anchor on the standard, not the deadline. Stratum has been building toward the Annex III substantive obligations — fundamental-rights impact assessment, technical documentation, human oversight, audit-grade logging — because those are the right engineering bar, not because August 2026 forced them. Compass should publish a short note this week so customers hear it from us before they hear it from counsel; Helm should consider a Briefing paragraph on "we ship for the standard, not the deadline."

02 L3 · Action recommended Regulatory · NYC AEDT · Enforcement · US

NYC AEDT: Comptroller calls enforcement "ineffective"; 2026 phase tightens.

The New York State Comptroller's December 2025 audit of DCWP's enforcement of Local Law 144 (AEDT) covering July 2023 to June 2025 concluded the regime is "ineffective" [4][5]. 75% of 311 calls about AEDT issues were misrouted and never reached DCWP; in a survey of 32 companies, DCWP identified one non-compliance case while the Comptroller's auditors identified at least 17 potential violations in the same set [4][5]. DCWP has committed to better complaint routing, cross-trained staff, more rigorous investigations, and proactive (not just complaint-driven) enforcement [4]. Multiple employment-law firms (DLA Piper, LegalClarity, NYC Bias Audit Co.) are now telegraphing a stricter 2026 enforcement phase with more frequent investigations [4][5]. Penalty structure: $500 first violation, $500 per additional same-day violation, with failing to conduct a bias audit producing $15,000–$45,000 per AEDT per month of non-compliance [5]. Fact: the Comptroller's report and the penalty math are public-record. Claim by DCWP: reformed enforcement is in flight. Inference: 2026-H2 will see the first wave of proactive AEDT enforcement, and the per-AEDT monthly meter is the buyer-facing number that will resonate.

Why this matters to Stratum

This is the proximate-pressure half of signal 01. With the EU AI Act Annex III deadline moved out 16 months, NYC AEDT and Mobley v. Workday are the two enforcement events that will shape buyer behavior through the end of 2026. Yesterday's Compass note (the 1.1B-rejections figure) covered Mobley; this signal covers the per-AEDT monthly meter. The buyer-side objection Compass should pre-empt is "the Comptroller said no one's enforcing it, so why pay for compliant tooling?" The numerically clean answer is: 75% of complaints were misrouted because the system was broken; the system is being fixed; once fixed, the meter runs at $15K–$45K per AEDT per month. Vector should weave this into the next Cordova (NYC-headquartered) and Halcyon (mixed-region) touches.

Stratum's bias-audit pipeline runs continuously, not annually." For the Mercator note, add only if Mercator has NYC operations on the org chart.

03 L2 · Watch Regulatory · EU AI Act · Consultation · Annex III

EC draft Annex III guidelines: consultation closes 2026-06-23.

On 2026-05-19, the European Commission published draft guidelines on classifying high-risk AI systems under the AI Act and opened a public consultation that closes 2026-06-23 [6][7][8]. The guidelines cover the eight Annex III areas — employment among them — and articulate the test the EC will use to decide whether a given AI system is "high-risk": if the system materially influences a decision affecting career prospects, livelihood, or rights, it is likely high-risk [7][8]. Fact: the draft is published and the consultation is open (Lewis Silkin, Hunton, McCann FitzGerald, DWF, Crowell & Moring). Claim by EC: the guidelines may be revised before adoption. Inference: this is the moment for HCM-AI vendors to influence the line between "decision-support that materially influences" and "decision-support that summarizes for a human" — the line that determines whether interview-summarization, candidate-ranking, and JD-generation are in scope. Stratum's posture on the candidate-ranking surface (Kernel's eval set treats it as decision-class) is more conservative than what the draft requires; commenting from that posture is a credibility play.

Why this matters to Stratum

Two reasons to engage. First, filing a public comment is a low-cost way to put Stratum on the record as a vendor that treats the standard as the bar — directly reinforcing signal 01's posture. Second, the consultation outcome will shape the post-2027-12-02 enforcement framing; the line the EC draws now is the line every Stratum customer will be measured against in 2028. The cost is one Forge/Kernel afternoon; the upside is a citable footprint in the regulatory record.
