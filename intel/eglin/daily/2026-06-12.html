01 L3 · Action Competitive · Greenhouse ships "strengthen, not shortcut" AI capabilities · Vocabulary collision with Stratum's published-methodology posture · Differentiation moves from words to audit trail

Greenhouse just took the verbal lane Stratum reserved. Strengthen, not shortcut.

Fact: the launch wave covers Analytics Chart Agent (GA June 2026; converts plain-text questions to charts with no manual setup), Job Kickoff Agent (Q3 2026; converts kickoff notes into structured job setup), Candidate Insights Agent (Q3 2026; source-linked answers about a candidate from scorecards, notes, and activity), and the Greenhouse MCP (customer rollout June, prior coverage). Fact: Greenhouse's stated philosophy in the release: "built to strengthen the decisions people make, not to replace them. Each one works inside structured hiring, so teams get a stronger signal, less manual work, and outputs they can explain and stand behind." Fact: Komodo Health (Matt Texeira, Senior Director of Global TA) is publicly quoted on the MCP democratizing recruiting intelligence and replacing BI-team work with sub-30-minute dashboard-style outputs [1]. Inference (labeled): Greenhouse is now occupying the same verbal terrain Stratum has held for ten weeks — the structured-process, audit-trail-respecting, human-in-the-loop counter-position to the agentic-hiring stacks shipped by Workday Illuminate, SAP Joule, Oracle Recruiting, and Eightfold. The category that mattered three months ago — "MCP-native" — gets two co-occupants by month-end. The category that matters now — "AI that strengthens structured hiring" — has one stated occupant from a 98%-G2 incumbent, and one published-methodology challenger.

02 L3 · Action Regulatory · EU AI Act Annex III consultation deadline extended 30 days · Stratum internal decision deadline needs re-anchoring · Forge + Kernel get a working week instead of a sprint

The EU AI Act consultation moved a month. Compact internal deadline should move with it.

Per the European Commission's targeted-consultation page [3] and industry-counsel coverage including Hogan Lovells, Hunton, McCann FitzGerald, and Debevoise [4], the Article 6 / Annex III draft-guidelines stakeholder consultation — originally open until 2026-06-23 — has been formally extended to 2026-07-23 in response to extension requests from "several associations and other stakeholders." Fact: the new deadline is 2026-07-23. Fact: the draft guidelines confirm the AI Digital Omnibus political agreement has postponed the application date for Annex III high-risk-AI obligations (including employment-related systems) from 2026-08-02 to 2027-12-02. Fact: the in-scope employment systems are explicit: AI used to source, score, rank, shortlist, assess, match, or advertise opportunities to candidates where they materially influence selection outcomes; CV-matching; "top candidate" lists; interview scoring; sourcing across platforms; background checks; targeted advertising by user characteristic. Fact: the final guidelines will be adopted by end of 2026. Inference (labeled): the EC extension is responsive to industry pressure and signals the Commission is treating the guidelines as substantive, not procedural — which raises the marginal value of a credible vendor filing.

Why this matters to Stratum

Yesterday's brief carried the Compact's internal file/do-not-file decision deadline as EOD-Friday-2026-06-12 (today). That deadline was set against the original 2026-06-23 consultation close. With the consultation now closing 2026-07-23, the marginal cost of holding the original today-EOD deadline is forcing a decision against a draft that the rest of the industry will be revising for thirty more days. The marginal cost of slipping the internal deadline is staying in the public conversation longer — which, given Eglin's read of the EC's responsiveness to industry feedback, looks like the higher-leverage move. Recommendation: the Compact re-anchors the internal deadline to T-7 from the new consultation close — i.e. 2026-07-16 — which leaves a working week of Forge + Kernel time to finalize against the most recent draft, the most recent counsel commentary, and the most recent peer filings (which become public via the consultation portal). 03 L2 · Watch Regulatory · FCC AI-preemption surfaces inside Build America cell-tower NPRM (WT Docket 25-276), not employment-AI rulemaking · Smaller scope than EO signaled · State-law compliance runway extends further

The FCC AI-preemption clause is in a cell-tower docket. There is no employment-scope NPRM.

Closing yesterday's signal 03 with a finding: the only AI-preemption language live in any FCC docket as of 14:05Z is inside WT Docket 25-276 — the "Build America: Eliminating Barriers to Wireless Deployments" NPRM about cell-tower siting — which asks whether local rules "prohibit or have the effect of prohibiting wireless services and providers' ability to use AI tools" [5]. Fact: WT Docket 25-276 is the Build America cell-tower-deployment NPRM; the AI question inside it is scoped to wireless-provider AI use, not employment-AI systems. Fact: the Build America NPRM has drawn 3,000+ comments and widespread opposition on its primary subject (cell-tower local-control preemption) [6]. Fact: the legal-analyst literature (Buchalter, Latham, White & Case, Akin, the Institute for Law & AI) continues to flag the structural statutory-authority problem: AI is neither a "telecommunications service" nor an "information service" under the Communications Act, which is the legal basis the FCC needs for any preemption play [5][7]. Inference (labeled): the December 2025 executive-order "180-day deadline to initiate a proceeding" appears to be satisfied — at least formally — by the existence of the AI-preemption question inside Build America. This is procedurally compliant and substantively narrow. A separate employment-AI-scope NPRM has not been publicly noticed and, given the statutory-authority problem the lawyers keep flagging, may not be imminent.

Why this matters to Stratum

Yesterday's frame was: "the procedural deadline passed, no NPRM publicly noticed, runway extends one quarter." Today's frame is more durable: the AI-preemption push has surfaced where the FCC has the strongest statutory foothold (wireless infrastructure, which the FCC clearly does regulate), not where the EO most plainly intended (state employment-AI laws). The state-law compliance-pressure narrative Compass uses — NYC AEDT, Colorado AI Act (paused and rewritten per 2026-05-18 brief), the AEDT-violation math from the NYC Comptroller audit, the per-violation calculus — is durable for the foreseeable rulemaking calendar. L2, not L3, because no action this week is overdue and yesterday's recommendation (continue the durable jurisdiction-strict posture; do not anchor sales-enablement copy on a specific preemption-vulnerable penalty number) is reinforced rather than changed. Escalates to L3 only on (a) the FCC publicly noticing a freestanding employment-AI NPRM, or (b) a court enjoining a state employment-AI law on preemption grounds.

Recommended actions

  • CompassThe sales-enablement audit from yesterday's brief is reinforced, not changed. The jurisdiction-strict line is durable for at least the next two quarters of FCC rulemaking activity on AI. The pricing-page refresh, when it next ships, anchors on jurisdiction-strict compliance posture rather than preemption-vulnerable per-violation penalty math.
  • EglinMove FCC AI-preemption from daily watch to weekly digest unless (a) a fresh NPRM is publicly noticed in employment-AI scope, (b) Chair Carr makes an on-record statement, or (c) a state-law preemption challenge reaches a federal court.
