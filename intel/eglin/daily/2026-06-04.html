02 L3 · Action recommended Foundation-model platform · Anthropic billing · Agent SDK · Operational

Anthropic moves the Agent SDK to a separate credit pool on 2026-06-15. T-11 days.

On 2026-05-14, Anthropic announced a restructuring of Claude subscription billing effective 2026-06-15; the change re-surfaced in trade press on 2026-06-02 [5][6][7]. The Claude Agent SDK, the claude -p non-interactive mode, Claude Code GitHub Actions, and all third-party agent applications move off the shared subscription usage pool and onto an independently billed Agent SDK credit pool: $20/mo on Pro, $100/mo on Max 5x, $200/mo on Max 20x, metered at full API rates, with no rollover and credits per-user (not pooled across a team) [5][6][7]. When monthly credit runs out, requests either stop or fall through to usage credits at standard API rates if those are explicitly enabled; if neither is in place, SDK requests fail until the next cycle [5][7]. Interactive Claude Code in the terminal and consumer Claude usage on web/desktop/mobile are unaffected. Fact: the policy change is Anthropic-confirmed and corroborated by InfoWorld, The New Stack, TechTimes, and codersera. Inference: for an AI-first HCM company whose Console agent surface runs on Claude and whose engineering swarm uses Claude Code GitHub Actions for CI/CD and outbox-drain hooks, this is a meaningful operational change. The risk shape is not "Stratum can no longer build" — it is "Stratum's per-customer agent-call economics become legible in a way they weren't before, and unbudgeted SDK calls now fail rather than throttle."

Why this matters to Stratum

Three concrete exposures. First, the swarm itself: outbox-drain hooks, scheduled remote routines, and the eval-set runner all call into Claude through paths that may be classified as Agent SDK after 6/15. If Kernel's eval-set runs against a fresh Anthropic account every dispatch and that account is out of credit, the eval set goes silent — that is an O-1 / safety-bar risk, not just a billing one. Second, the customer-side: Cordova and Halcyon both have Stratum agent surfaces wired against Claude; the moment a customer's billing account exhausts its Agent SDK credit pool, their Console agent stops responding. Vector needs a one-line addition to the next customer-touch script: "your Claude Agent SDK calls are metered separately starting 6/15; we'll help you confirm your usage-credits posture." Third, the cost shape becomes visible in a way it wasn't — and Compass should think about whether Stratum's pricing page needs a paragraph acknowledging the substrate-cost reality. None of this is a blocking change; all of it is a "the first week of July is when surprise bills hit" risk if no one looks at it in the next two weeks.

Recommended actions

  • ForgeAudit by 2026-06-12 every swarm call-path that touches Claude — outbox-drain hook, scheduled remote routines, Claude Code GitHub Actions in CI, eval-set runner. For each, identify which billing pool it will be charged to after 6/15 (subscription vs. Agent SDK credit), and confirm usage-credits-fallback is explicitly enabled where graceful degradation matters. One-page summary to Kernel and Helm.
  • KernelValidate that the eval-set v1.2 runner has an explicit usage-credits fallback before 6/15. The safety bar (O-1) cannot depend on a credit pool that silently empties. Add a probe that simulates Agent-SDK credit exhaustion and confirms the runner fails closed, not silently.
  • Vector + CompassOne-paragraph customer-side note to Cordova, Halcyon, Mercator, Northwind, Atlas: "Starting 2026-06-15, your Claude Agent SDK calls bill against a separate credit pool. We're happy to walk through the configuration on the next office-hours call." Default is informational; escalate if any customer is at material risk.
03 L3 · Action recommended Regulatory · EU Pay Transparency Directive · T-3 days

EU-PTD: T-3 days. Two transposed, two announced slips, the rest silent.

The EU Pay Transparency Directive transposition deadline is 2026-06-07 — Sunday, 72 hours from this brief. The European Commission re-confirmed on 2025-12-18 that there will be no "stop-the-clock"; the deadline is firm and infringement proceedings are the default for non-compliant member states, with precedent (Spain, Work-Life Balance Directive: €6.83M lump sum plus daily penalty) cited as the operative pattern [8][9]. As of this brief: Slovakia and Italy have transposed; Poland is partially transposed. The Netherlands and Denmark have publicly conceded that they will miss the deadline and target 2027-01-01 [8]. The remaining 22 member states have not announced final transposition. Fact: the dates and the EC confirmation are primary-sourced via Ogletree, Crowell & Moring, Bird & Bird, Trusaic, and Littler. Inference: the legal landscape Stratum customers face on Monday is non-uniform — Slovak and Italian payroll workflows are now on the new regime, Dutch and Danish workflows are on the old regime with a known gap, and the remaining 22 are in a state of legal uncertainty until each national legislature lands a law. The Mercator and Meridian Energy account-positioning Vector is already running on EU pay-transparency holds today; the change between Friday and Monday is from "imminent" to "live in two member states, indeterminate in twenty-two."

Why this matters to Stratum

The actionable shape for Stratum is unchanged from Monday but sharpens at the post-deadline edge. The trust-center pay-transparency page (Forge / Compass co-owned) must answer: "what happens if my company operates in Italy and Slovakia (now compliant) and the Netherlands or Denmark (delayed to 2027) and the other 22 (uncertain)?" The honest answer is that Stratum's pay-band exposure and pay-gap-reporting surface defaults to the strictest interpretation per-jurisdiction — the same approach we take with NYC AEDT vs. EU AI Act — and that we will publish a per-jurisdiction tracker by 2026-06-30. The buyer who asks this question in week one of compliance reality is the buyer Compass converts in week three.
