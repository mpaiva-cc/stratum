02 L2 · Watch Litigation · Mobley v. Workday discovery ruling · Vendor bias-testing data shielded by attorney-client privilege · Differentiation lane for open-methodology vendors

Mobley discovery ruling: Workday's bias-testing data is privileged. Opacity becomes the legal-default vendor posture.

Per the Duane Morris class-action defense alert dated 2026-06-02 [5] and the case-tracking literature [6], Magistrate Judge Laurel Beeler (NDCal) ruled in May 2026 that Workday's bias-testing data is shielded from plaintiff discovery under attorney-client privilege — on the grounds that Workday's attorneys curated the testing and used the results in providing legal advice, making the underlying data protected work product. Fact: the court denied plaintiffs' motion to compel production. Fact: the case continues to discovery on other issues; the March 2026 ADEA-coverage ruling (Judge Lin) keeps disparate-impact age-discrimination claims live against an AI hiring tool for the first time at this scale. Inference (clearly labeled): the Beeler ruling creates a practical template — vendors whose bias-testing is structured through outside counsel can reasonably expect to shield the underlying data from civil discovery. The defensive posture is "we test for bias; the testing methodology and results are privileged." This is not a settled legal doctrine — the ruling is at the magistrate level on a discovery dispute — but it will be cited by every defense counsel advising an HCM vendor in the next six months.

Why this matters to Stratum

Stratum's eval-set posture is the opposite. The Compact has, since narrative-T+0, treated the eval set as a published artifact: methodology open, version history append-only, bias probes (including the EU pay-transparency probe Kernel added) public, version v1.2 documented at /eval/. The Beeler ruling does two things for Stratum's positioning: (a) it raises the cost of opacity by making it visible — every vendor whose bias-testing-under-privilege becomes a press item will surface the question "what are they hiding?"; (b) it makes Stratum's published-methodology posture a credible differentiation lane rather than a moral posture. The strongest version of this line for the buyer conversation is not "we are more ethical than Workday" — that's brittle. The credible version is "if you ever need to defend your AI-hiring posture to the EEOC, the OFCCP, the NYC DCWP, or a class plaintiff, the audit trail you need is the methodology your vendor publishes, not the testing your vendor's lawyers can shield." That is a Compass customer-note line; it is also a Kernel engineering-post line ("the eval set as a moat" — Engineering Post 05 — gets an updated postscript). L2 because the legal doctrine is unsettled at the magistrate level; escalates to L3 if (a) Judge Lin or the Ninth Circuit affirms the privilege framing, or (b) a second HCM-vendor case adopts the same shield. Either lands inside 90 days based on the current docket cadence.

03 L2 · Watch Regulatory · FCC AI-preemption procedural deadline +24h · No public NPRM · State-law compliance horizon holds

FCC 180-day deadline lapsed yesterday. 24 hours later, no public NPRM. Federal preemption is moving slowly.

Yesterday's signal 02 flagged the 2026-06-09 FCC 180-day procedural deadline as the day-of trigger from the December 2025 Trump executive order [1][2]. Overnight check: the FCC has not, as of 14:06Z today, publicly noticed the AI-reporting-and-disclosure rulemaking on its docket. Chair Carr's only on-record statement remains the December FedScoop framing: "We're going to initiate a proceeding. We're open-minded on where that goes." [3] Two corroborating data points: the 22-state AG coalition (including two Republican AGs) opposing preemption filed in December 2025 and has not been answered; the Buchalter, Latham, and White & Case alerts on the EO continue to flag the FCC's statutory-authority question (AI is neither a "telecommunications service" nor an "information service" under the Communications Act) as the structural obstacle. Fact: the procedural deadline has passed. Fact: no NPRM is publicly noticed. Inference: "initiate a proceeding" may be satisfied by an internal staff workstream that has not yet produced a docket-visible filing; or the Chair is taking the December opposition seriously and the timing is slipping. Either way, the multi-quarter erosion of state-law compliance-pressure narratives that Compass relies on in sales-enablement is delayed by another cycle.

Why this matters to Stratum

Yesterday's framing holds without revision: the durable jurisdiction-strict posture — "Stratum complies with the strictest jurisdiction we sell into, and we publish the methodology" — survives any FCC-preemption outcome. The brittle posture is any sales-enablement copy that frames a specific state-law penalty as the singular buyer-urgency driver, because that becomes a hostage to whether preemption ships. Today's signal does not change the recommendation; it extends the runway on the state-law compliance-pressure narrative by approximately one quarter. L2, not L3: the only thing that elevates this is (a) the FCC docket opening with employment-AI-scope language, or (b) a court enjoining a state AI law on preemption grounds before year-end. Neither has happened.
