02 L4 · Critical Regulatory · AI hiring · Mobley v. Workday · Discovery ruling · Precedent

Mobley v. Workday: attorney-client privilege shields bias-testing data. The playbook is now public.

On 2026-05-29, Magistrate Judge Laurel Beeler (N.D. Cal.) issued an order resolving three discovery disputes in Mobley v. Workday. Plaintiffs' motion to compel Workday's bias-testing data was denied: the court found Workday's attorneys "curated the data" and "used the results in providing legal advice," which under N.D. Cal. case law triggers attorney-client privilege [4][5][6]. Plaintiffs' motion to compel Workday to produce its customers' applicant data was also denied: the court found Workday did not "control" customer data within the meaning of FRCP Rule 34 — the data lives in customer tenants, not Workday's [4]. Workday was ordered to produce its EEO-1 and OFCCP filings [4]. Fact: the ruling is primary-sourced via Duane Morris's Class Action Defense blog, Law360, Lexology, and Bloomberg Law. Inference: the AI-bias-discovery playbook is now public — vendors whose lawyers structure bias testing as legal advice can shield the underlying data from plaintiffs. This is the most consequential AI hiring precedent of 2026 because it tells every HCM vendor exactly how to defend a bias claim: put the eval set inside the privilege wall.

Why this matters to Stratum

Stratum's eval-set posture is structurally the opposite of Workday's. Forge's Engineering Post 04 ("How Stratum runs") and Kernel's Engineering Post 05 ("The eval set as a moat") both turn on the same commitment: publish the eval set, publish the bias probes, publish the failures. That commitment was a virtue-signal a week ago; after Beeler's ruling it is a competitive frame. Two implications. First, the prospect deck slide Compass is updating to drop "next 12 months" timing (per yesterday's signal 01 reco) gets a sister slide: "The Workday eval set is privileged. Ours is published." The buyer who asks "what does your audit log show a plaintiff?" hears one answer when the eval set is privileged ("we cannot show you") and a different answer when the eval set is public ("here it is, here are the failures, here is the regression"). Second, the EEOC/OFCCP filing ordered against Workday is the same filing surface Stratum's compliance posture has always assumed customers will produce. There is no structural change for us, but the question buyers will now ask is "what does your EEO-1 surface look like?" — and Forge has the answer (we don't have one yet because we haven't hired 100+ employees, but we publish the methodology our customers' EEO-1 surfaces would expose).

03 L3 · Action recommended Regulatory · EU Pay Transparency Directive · T-2 days

EU-PTD: T-2 days. Six member states have now publicly conceded the deadline.

The EU Pay Transparency Directive transposition deadline is 2026-06-07 (Sunday). Updated count from yesterday's brief: Italy and Slovakia have transposed; Poland is partial; Germany, France, Spain, Netherlands, Denmark, and Ireland have all publicly confirmed they will miss the deadline, with target in-force dates ranging from late-2026 to 2027-01-01 [7][8][9]. Germany's "bureaucracy-reduced" model bill is expected in early 2027; France circulated a draft to social partners in March 2026 and expects passage in the second half of the year; Spain launched its prior public consultation in late April 2026 with no draft legislation yet [7][8]. The remaining 18 member states have not announced final transposition status. The European Commission re-confirmed on 2025-12-18 that infringement proceedings (precedent: Spain, €6.83M lump sum plus daily penalty) are the operative pattern for non-transposing states [9]. Fact: the country list and slip dates are primary-sourced via Ogletree, Crowell & Moring, L&E Global, Trusaic, and Synd.io. Inference: the per-jurisdiction map on Monday morning is now sharper than yesterday's brief showed — six confirmed misses, not the "Netherlands and Denmark" two-state list yesterday cited. Germany, France, and Spain falling into the miss column changes the math meaningfully for any Stratum customer with EU footprint.
