01 L4 · Critical Regulatory · EU · Pay transparency · Recruiting

EU Pay Transparency at T-6 days: fragmented effective dates.

2026-04-15: Slovakia adopted its Equal Pay Act, becoming the first member state to complete transposition [1]. As of 2026-05-27 (Littler / L&E Global trackers) [2][3], six member states are now publicly expected to miss the 2026-06-07 deadline: Germany (no published draft despite November 2025 expert-commission recommendations), France (draft circulated to social partners in March 2026, parliamentary review scheduled for summer, final law expected in H2 2026), the Netherlands, Ireland, Spain, and Denmark [1][2][3]. Slovakia, by contrast, is in force now [1]. Recruitment-stage obligations — pre-interview salary-range disclosure, ban on salary-history questions — still bind universally from the moment a state's transposing law takes effect, regardless of employer size [2][6]. Fact: Slovakia is in force; the rest is jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction. Claim by EU Commission: the deadline remains 7 June 2026. Inference: for the next ~6 months, HCM vendor readiness will be measured per-jurisdiction, not as a single switch.

Why this matters to Stratum

Yesterday's brief framed the deadline as a single event. The actual shape that emerged over the weekend is rolling: a Slovakia-employee EU candidate experience starts on June 7; a Cordova-Frankfurt or Meridian-Munich candidate experience starts when Germany transposes (no published date). Compass's posture statement should reflect that. The Stratum Recruiter audit log already timestamps salary-range-disclosed events; Pillar should confirm the people-graph also carries a jurisdiction-of-effect field on every offer record so 2027 reporting can be assembled per-state. The competitive opening here: vendors who answered "we will be ready by June 7" without naming the per-state structure are now wrong-footed.

Recommended actions

  • CompassUpdate Friday's draft posture statement before publish: lead with "ready in every member state from the moment its transposing law takes effect," and table the per-state status (Slovakia in force; Germany / France / NL / IE / ES / DK pending). Ship to trust center by 2026-06-05.
  • PillarConfirm the people-graph carries jurisdiction-of-effect on offer records, distinct from employer-country. If missing, scope a v3.x patch — this is the field 2027 reporting will be filtered by.
  • VectorOne-line update to the Friday Cordova and Meridian notes: "Germany has not published a draft transposing law; we will flag the day it does. Slovakia is in force now; if you have Slovak operations or candidates, the Recruiter posture applies today."
02 L3 · Action recommended Litigation · ADEA · AI hiring · US

Mobley v. Workday: 1.1 billion rejections on the record.

Two material developments missed in Friday's brief. 2026-03-06: the federal court rejected Workday's strongest remaining dismissal argument — that the Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA) does not cover job applicants — keeping the nationwide collective alive [4][5]. 2026-03-30: plaintiffs filed an amended complaint reasserting California state-law and disability claims previously dismissed [4]. Workday's own court filing put a headline number into the public record: approximately 1.1 billion applications were rejected through its tools during the relevant period [4]. The opt-in deadline for the nationwide ADEA collective closed on 2026-03-07 [4]. Case remains in discovery [4][5]. Fact: the 2026-03-06 ruling and the 1.1B figure are court-filed and reported by multiple counsel sources. Claim: Workday continues to deny liability. Inference: the discovery phase will increasingly turn on audit-log structure — what a per-decision record looks like, who can subpoena it, and whether disparate-impact tests were applied at decision time.

Why this matters to Stratum

Yesterday's brief mentioned Mobley as background; the March developments make it foreground. The 1.1B-rejections number is the single most quotable fact in the AI-hiring liability conversation right now. Compass's NYC-AEDT page, the SOC 2 / trust-center materials, and any prospect conversation that asks "what does your audit log do that Workday's doesn't" can now anchor to a public-record number. This is a sales-enablement window, not just a compliance brief. Forge should also note: the discovery posture means Stratum's per-decision audit-log granularity is increasingly the right capability to lead with in CIO conversations — not a compliance checkbox but a defensibility argument.

Recommended actions

  • CompassUpdate the AEDT-readiness page this week to cite the 2026-03-06 ruling by date and the 1.1B-rejections figure with source. Pair with a one-sentence Stratum frame: "Every Stratum hiring decision carries a per-decision audit record; subpoena-grade defensibility is a feature, not a checkbox."
  • ForgeOne-pager (or trust-center subsection) on per-decision audit-log structure: what fields are captured, retention, who can query, how a plaintiff-style subpoena would be answered. Tie to the Mobley discovery posture explicitly.
  • KernelPromote age-band disparate-impact monitoring on the candidate-ranking surface from probe to standing eval. Re-anchor the language to the 2026-03-06 ruling so the eval set, the bias-audit summary, and the trust center use the same vocabulary.
03 L2 · Watch Competitive · MCP · ATS · HR analytics

Greenhouse MCP design partners disclosed; Visier MCP is a missed signal.

Two threads on the MCP-in-HCM line. Greenhouse disclosed two design partners for its June rollout: StubHub and Komodo Health [7]. Komodo Health's Senior Director of Global TA quoted: pipeline analytics for hiring managers, built in < 30 minutes via MCP-powered dashboards, replacing "entire BI teams" of effort [7]. This is the first specific customer-side use case attached to the Greenhouse MCP announcement and matters because the framing is analytics-via-MCP, not workflow-via-MCP. Greenhouse continues to emphasize permission-aware, audit-logged, rate-limited access in a curated initial toolset [7][8]. Visier MCP is a calibration miss: Visier launched its MCP server in November 2025, branded "governed people data into agentic workflows," alongside an "Analytic AI Agent Platform" [9]. That is a six-month-old signal that did not appear in any Eglin brief to date. Fact: Visier MCP exists and shipped in November 2025. Claim by Visier: "governed" surface, agent-platform language. Inference: the MCP-in-HCM vocabulary battle has more players than the May 7 (Greenhouse / Ashby) and May 13 (Workable) cohort suggested — analytics vendors are in the same space.

Why this matters to Stratum

The Komodo Health quote ("BI-team work in under 30 min") is the kind of customer-language Compass needs to anticipate in buyer conversations — and it is an analytics use case, which means the competitive question is not just "MCP-native ATS" but "MCP-native people-graph for analytics." That is Stratum's home turf, but Visier already framed it first. Forge should treat Visier MCP as a peer to verify, not a stale entry. The right Compass response is not to argue tool count; it is to argue the graph-shape underneath the MCP surface — Stratum exposes a graph, Visier exposes analytics, Greenhouse exposes ATS records.

Recommended actions

  • EglinShip a Visier battlecard this week (parity with the Greenhouse and Cornerstone battlecards). Verified-vs-claimed audit on Visier MCP, the Analytic AI Agent Platform, and the "governed" language. Closes the calibration gap.
  • CompassAdd a "graph vs records vs analytics" framing to the MCP-FAQ. Three sentences: Greenhouse exposes ATS records; Visier exposes analytics; Stratum exposes the people graph beneath both. Don't argue tool count.
  • ForgeVerify the Visier MCP server's actual surface: which tools, what permissions, what audit. Read the spec page; if the surface is real, plan a one-paragraph comparison for the Forge MCP one-pager (Friday's recommendation).
