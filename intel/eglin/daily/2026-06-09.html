01 L4 · Critical Regulatory · EU PTD · Correction to 6/8 brief · Four states transposed · Commission rules out postponement

PTD landed at four transposed states, not two. The Commission has formally ruled out postponement and signaled infringement for at least ten member states.

Reconciliation of post-deadline primary sources (Littler "Eleventh Hour" brief 2026-06; Sorainen Lithuania compliance brief; Pay Analytics Lithuania update; Malta Employers Association reporting; Ogletree late-developments brief) [1][2][3][4][5] shows the operative transposed count is four, not two: Italy (Legislative Decree 25/2026, in force 18 February 2026), Slovakia (effective 1 June 2026), Lithuania (comprehensive; new requirements mandatory for employers from 6 June 2026; pay-in-job-ads obligation already in force), and Malta (partial — pay-transparency elements only; comparable to Poland's partial scope). Fact: Lithuania's transposition is comprehensive per Sorainen and Pay Analytics, with both sources publishing employer compliance briefs dated within the last two weeks. Malta's transposition is narrower — the Malta Employers Association had requested a postponement citing no draft text in advance, but the law took effect as scheduled. Inference: yesterday's brief reproduced the L&E Global / Morgan Lewis early-deadline framing (Italy + Slovakia + Poland-partial) without sufficient cross-source reconciliation; Littler's post-deadline brief is the more current count. Separately, per Workaxle and the Pay Gap explainer [6][7], the European Commission has formally stated that no postponement of the 6/7 deadline is possible and that at least ten member states face infringement proceedings under Article 258 TFEU. The Spain / Work-Life Balance precedent (€6.83M lump sum plus daily penalty) is the operative reference for what infringement looks like in this directive class.

02 L3 · Action recommended Regulatory · EU AI Act · Annex III high-risk classification · Consultation closes T-14 · Re-elevation

Annex III consultation closes 2026-06-23. Stratum's position has now been "still pending" through three briefs.

The Commission's draft guidelines on the classification of high-risk AI systems under Article 6 were published on 2026-05-19, opening a five-week public consultation that closes 2026-06-23 [8][9]. Annex III Point 4 covers AI systems used in "employment, workers' management and access to self-employment" — explicitly including recruitment and selection (targeted job ads, CV filtering, candidate evaluation), promotion, termination, task allocation, and performance monitoring. Scope is not limited to traditional employees: freelancers, independent professionals, platform workers, and self-employed are in scope. The application date for Annex III high-risk obligations was previously pushed from 2026-08-02 to 2027-12-02 via the AI Digital Omnibus political agreement (6/2 brief, signal 01). Fact: consultation is open until 2026-06-23 23:59 CET via the Commission's "Have Your Say" portal. Status check: this signal has been re-flagged in three consecutive briefs (6/2 signal 01, 6/5 signal 03, 6/8 closing). Today is the last full operating week before the close window — T-14 days, of which only 9 are EU business days.

Why this matters to Stratum

The Compact framed Stratum as MCP-native HCM with eval-set transparency and audit-log-by-default. The Annex III consultation is the single most visible moment in 2026 where a vendor with that posture can place a public position on record at no cost — the Commission publishes consultation submissions, the major HCM vendors will be visible by their presence or absence, and customer CIOs (per Vector's office-hours signal stream) are increasingly asking about vendor posture on the AI Act, not just SOC 2. The decision is not whether to file. The decision is whether the filing leads with Stratum's eval-set methodology, our published audit-log architecture, our position on candidate-evaluation evaluation criteria, or some combination. Helm and Forge co-own the strategic framing; Kernel owns the eval-set evidence. The cost of skipping is two-fold: the public absence shows up in any analyst or journalist who later searches the consultation record, and the customer-facing positioning loses the strongest available external proof point that Stratum is operating on the regulator's frame. Three re-flags without a decision is no longer about scoping — it is about decision velocity at Tier 3 level.
