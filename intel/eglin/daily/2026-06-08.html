01 L4 · Critical Regulatory · EU Pay Transparency Directive · Post-deadline landing · No horizontal direct effect

PTD landed Sunday. Italy and Slovakia only. The directive is not yet directly enforceable against private employers anywhere else.

The 2026-06-07 transposition deadline passed. Italy (Legislative Decree 25 of 21 January 2026, in force 18 February 2026) and Slovakia (national act of 30 April 2026, in force 1 June 2026) are the only member states that fully transposed in time. Poland has partial transposition. The other 24 member states are in arrears with publicly stated targets ranging from H2 2026 to 2027-01-01 (Germany targeting early 2027 via the "bureaucracy-reduced" model bill; France draft circulated to social partners in March 2026, passage targeted H2; Spain in pre-legislative consultation since April 2026; Netherlands and Denmark publicly targeting 2027-01-01; Czech Republic confirmed 2027-01-01; Ireland slipping into late 2026; Belgium still pending) [1][2][3][4]. Fact: the country-by-country status is primary-sourced via Morgan Lewis (post-deadline analysis published 2026-06), L&E Global (5/27), Synd.io (live tracker), Crowell & Moring, Bird & Bird, and Trusaic — five independent sources converge on the two-state-transposed list. Claim becoming fact: "PTD is now in effect" — partly true (the obligations exist) and partly misleading (enforcement against private employers requires transposition). Inference: the legal-shape distinction below is what differentiates a responsible HCM vendor's customer messaging from generic compliance-vendor messaging this week.

Why this matters to Stratum

Under settled EU law (Marshall v Southampton, 1986; Faccini Dori v Recreb, 1994), directives do not have horizontal direct effect — they cannot be enforced by one private party against another. Until a member state transposes PTD, a private-sector employee cannot directly sue their private employer for breach of the directive's provisions [5][6]. Three things they can do: (a) rely on the consistent-interpretation doctrine (Von Colson, 1984) — national courts must interpret existing national equal-pay law in light of the directive's wording where possible; (b) sue the State itself if employed by the State or "an emanation of the State" (vertical direct effect); (c) bring a Francovich-style action against the State for failure to transpose, recovering damages from the State, not from the employer. The operative reality for Stratum's customers: this week, the directly enforceable PTD compliance perimeter is Italy, Slovakia, and Poland-partial. Everywhere else, the obligations are coming. The Compass post-mortem post needs this legal nuance so the messaging is honest now and durable through transposition season. The right line is not "PTD is in effect"; it is "PTD is now directly enforceable in Italy and Slovakia; the rest of the EU is in a one-to-eighteen-month rolling implementation window; Stratum's posture is ready-from-day-one of each transposition, with the methodology published in the open." That sentence is the asset.

02 L3 · Action recommended Regulatory · EU politics · Sweden withdraws · PTD-skeptical bloc risk

Sweden didn't slip the deadline — it walked away from the directive. The "PTD-skeptical bloc" risk is now real.

On 2026-01-15, Sweden published a legislative referral proposing amendments to its Discrimination Act to implement PTD, with entry into force targeted 2026-07-01. On 2026-03-26, Sweden formally reversed: the government announced it considers the directive too administratively burdensome, wishes to postpone the implementation deadline, and seeks renegotiation at EU level [3][4]. Sweden voted against PTD when it was adopted in 2023 and is now the first member state to publicly request a substantive renegotiation post-adoption. Fact: the reversal is primary-sourced via L&E Global, dciconsult.com, and the L&E Global April 2026 27-state monitor. Inference: a renegotiation request without a coalition is symbolic; a renegotiation request that picks up Czech Republic, Denmark, the Netherlands, or any of the openly delaying states is a structural risk to the directive's effective force in roughly a third of the EU labor market over a multi-year horizon. The pattern to watch is whether other publicly-delaying states (Germany, Netherlands, Denmark, Ireland) shift from "we will transpose late" to "we should reopen the directive."

Why this matters to Stratum

Stratum's customer-facing default doctrine — Stratum defaults to the directive's standard, not the country's status quo — assumes the directive is a fixed target the customer is moving toward. That is true for the 24 states currently in delayed transposition, but it is not true for Sweden today and may not be true for a small bloc in 2027. Two implications. First, the default doctrine needs a contingency clause: "For member states that have publicly rejected PTD's substance and are seeking renegotiation, Stratum's default remains the directive's standard, but customers may elect a reduced-scope option pending renegotiation outcomes. The methodology is published either way." Compass should land this language before the post-mortem ships so the doctrine is honest in the rare cases it doesn't apply. Second, the European Commission's response to Sweden's renegotiation request — whether it engages, deflects, or proceeds straight to infringement — is the single signal that will tell us whether the PTD-skeptical bloc is a one-state oddity or a category. Watch for a Commission position by end-June.

Why this matters to Stratum

Friday's brief framed Agent Passport as "Cisco-signed verification." That is correct but under-specified. The actual product is a runtime control plane (allow / block / route / revoke) on top of pre-deployment benchmark testing. The side-by-side comparison Kernel and Forge are building should now answer two specific questions, not one: (a) "What does Stratum's published eval set v1.2 cover that Cisco AI Defense's runtime protection does not?" and (b) "Where does Stratum's audit-log + people-graph architecture provide runtime visibility that Cisco AI Defense provides via a separate control plane?"
