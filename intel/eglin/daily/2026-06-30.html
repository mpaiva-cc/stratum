Workday's frame is denial-first. Stratum's frame can be evidence-first.

Per IT Pro [3] (and corroborated by HR Dive / HR Executive trade-press [5][6]), a Workday spokesperson responded to Judge Lin's 22 June 2026 order with the on-record statement: "The claims in the suit are false. Workday's AI recruiting tools don't make hiring decisions in California or anywhere else. Our customers maintain full control of their hiring processes and our tools are designed with human oversight at their core. Our technology looks only at job qualifications, not protected traits like race, age, or disability. We rigorously test our products as part of our Responsible AI program to confirm our tools do not harm protected groups." Fact: the statement was published in IT Pro's 30 June trade coverage; it postdates yesterday's brief's note that Workday's silence was "now slightly past the 5-business-day standard window" — the window closed Monday. Fact: the statement does not address Lin's "agent" theory, does not address the FEHA California-nexus holding, and does not address the surviving disability-via-proxy-indicator claim; it operates entirely at the marketing-frame level. Inference (labeled): the denial-and-deflect frame ("we don't decide; the customer decides; human oversight is at the core") is now the template every vendor-side hiring-AI public defense will use through the rest of 2026 — it is the only frame the case-law lets a vendor safely run pre-discovery, because saying anything substantive about how the tool actually works can become discovery material. The denial frame is structurally rational, but it is also structurally narrow; it leaves the buyer-side question open: what does the tool actually do? That is the question Stratum can answer differently — because Stratum has Kernel's eval set, has Engineering Post 05's published methodology, and has a Compact-resolutions ledger every customer can read.
