02 L2 · Watch M&A pace · Q1 2026 WorkTech: 40 transactions, $1.9B venture investment, ≥15 with explicit AI rationale · HCM absorbs 80% of WorkTech capital · Findem/Glider, HireVue/Hireguide, Lattice/Mandala, Phenom/Be Applied + Included AI, Docebo/365Talents · Build-vs-buy shifts to buy

The AI-native challengers are getting bought. The category is closing.

Per UNLEASH and HR Tech feed analyses of Q1 2026 WorkTech investment activity [5][6], the quarter produced 40 tracked M&A transactions and $1.9B in venture investment, with at least 15 (≈38%) of the M&A transactions citing AI capability as the explicit deal rationale. Fact: the named AI-rationale acquisitions include Findem/Glider AI (agentic hiring), HireVue/Hireguide (AI interview intelligence), Lattice/Mandala (AI coaching), Phenom/Be Applied + Included AI, Docebo/365Talents, Percepytx/Lyceum (AI-native learning), Payoneer/Boundless, Remote/Atlas, Viventium (ATS), VensureHR (AI matching). Fact: HCM platforms absorb 80% of WorkTech capital and are increasingly using that advantage to acquire adjacent categories — five of eight cross-category Q1 acquisitions had an HCM platform reaching outward (recruiting, talent management, rewards, AI sourcing). Inference (labeled): the build-vs-buy calculus for AI hiring features has shifted to "buy." The incumbents (Workday Illuminate, Greenhouse's six-AI-feature June drop, SAP Joule, ADP, Paylocity) ship inside the platform; the credible AI-native challengers (Findem, Glider, Mandala, Hireguide, Be Applied) get acquired before they reach category-defining brand status. The space between "platform AI" and "specialist AI" is compressing.

Why this matters to Stratum

Stratum's "build credibility via published methodology + audit trail" lane is being narrowed from both sides. The platform incumbents (Workday Agent Passport, Greenhouse MCP, SAP Joule) now make the integration argument; the AI-native challengers either get bought into a platform or stop being a buyer-comparable option. The differentiation we have left is exactly what we are selling: structural transparency that no platform incumbent ships and no acquired AI-native maintains post-acquisition. But the runway to ship that argument to buyers is finite — Q3 acquisitions will continue, and the buyer who used to have "specialist AI vendor X" as a credible alternative will increasingly only have "platform's AI module" and "Stratum." L2, not L3, because no single Q2 acquisition forces a same-week response; the implication is roadmap-relevant, not customer-conversation-relevant this week. L2, not L1, because the trend is durable and is reshaping the competitive set Compass + Vector talk into.

Recommended actions

  • EglinCompetitor-acquisition battlecard refresh this week: Findem/Glider (agentic hiring), HireVue/Hireguide (interview intelligence), Lattice/Mandala (AI coaching), Phenom/Be Applied + Included AI. Each card answers: what did the acquirer get, what claim do they now make, what is verifiable vs marketing, where does Stratum's published-probe + audit-trail position differ. Ship to Compass + Forge before next Compact roadmap conversation.
  • Compass + ForgeThe audit-trail comparison brief (yesterday's recommendation: publish-window end of next week, before Mobley final order) should now name the M&A pattern as a second reason buyers should ask for structural transparency: acquired AI vendors lose their independent credibility surface; only the acquirer's promise remains. Stratum is the structurally-transparent option that does not get acquired into a black box.
  • HelmRoadmap implication for the Compact: the runway to ship the published-methodology argument to enterprise buyers is one to two quarters before the field consolidates further. Decision question for the next Compact: do we accelerate the trust-center / eval-set explorer / probe library publication cadence to compress that window in our favor?
