01 L3 · Action Regulatory · Mobley v. Workday FEHA oral argument 6/15 · Judge Lin's tentative reaches Workday as "employment agency" under California state law nationwide · State employment-AI law extraterritoriality expands · Pairs with Friday's federal-preemption-narrows signal

California FEHA just reached across the country. Through Workday.

Per MLex, Courthouse News, Akin Gump's AI Law and Regulation Tracker, and HR Executive's same-day coverage [1][2][3], U.S. District Judge Rita Lin (N.D. Cal.) heard oral argument on 2026-06-15 on her 2026-05-27 tentative ruling in Mobley v. Workday, Inc., case 3:23-cv-00770. Fact: the tentative position is that California's Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA) reaches Workday's AI hiring tools because Workday itself performs FEHA-regulated activity on the employer's behalf — making Workday directly liable as an "employment agency" under California state law, independent of where the applicant lives or where the employer is headquartered. Fact: Mobley filed in 2025 alleging Workday's AI screening discriminated on race, age, and disability across 80+ rejected applications. Fact: Judge Lin previously granted conditional certification (5/16/2025) for a nationwide ADEA collective action covering applicants 40+ screened by Workday's AI since September 2020. Fact: Workday argued at the 6/15 hearing that FEHA cannot reach conduct where neither the employer nor the applicant is in California; Lin's tentative reading is that Workday's own conduct is the FEHA-regulated act, regardless of employer/applicant geography. Inference (labeled): if the tentative survives oral argument unchanged, California state employment-AI law becomes the de facto national floor for any AI hiring tool whose vendor operates in California — and Workday, Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever, iCIMS, and Stratum's MCP-native ATS all qualify. The reading is durable: Lin issued the tentative after full briefing, and the "employment agency" framing reaches the vendor regardless of customer geography.

Why this matters to Stratum

Friday's Signal 01 read the Great American AI Act's explicit deployment-carve-out as federal-text confirmation that state employment-AI laws are durable. Today's Mobley signal is the operational expansion of the same lane: California's FEHA is now (per the tentative) reaching the AI hiring vendor across state lines, not just the in-California employer. The two signals compose: federal preemption is narrowing, state law is broadening through judicial interpretation. Stratum's competitive position: the jurisdiction-strict compliance posture Compass has anchored on since the 5/29 brief is no longer the cautious read — it is the federally-text-supported and judicially-expanding baseline. Stratum's published bias-probe set (NYC AEDT, Illinois AIVII, Colorado ADMT, California ADMT, EU pay-transparency) is now the demonstrable proof point for the exact compliance question the Mobley case puts to every vendor: "show me the artifact, not the policy." L3, not L4, because the tentative is not yet final and oral argument may produce modifications; L3, not L2, because the comparison brief Compass + Forge are drafting (per Friday's recommendation) must integrate the Mobley reading before publication. If Stratum publishes the brief without it, the brief is stale on day one.

02 L2 · Watch Analyst · Josh Bersin Company "HR 2030: The Agentic HR Blueprint" released 6/8 · 30–50% HR headcount reduction prediction by 2030 · 95–130 specialized agents architecture · Buyer frame Stratum will encounter through Q3

Bersin just put a number on it. HR shrinks 30–50% by 2030.

Per the Josh Bersin Company's release on 2026-06-08 [4][5], the firm published HR 2030: The Agentic HR Blueprint, a framework describing what the analyst calls "the largest HR transformation in decades." Fact: headline prediction — HR departments will shrink by 30 to 50 percent in headcount by 2030, with agentic AI automating core people processes (talent acquisition, internal mobility, learning, workforce planning, employee service). Fact: the blueprint architecture identifies 95 agent capabilities across the employee lifecycle and describes an architecture of up to 130 specialized agents working across HR, organized into five agent classes (employee, decision, monitoring, action, and business-rule agents) with "superagents" coordinating across them. Fact: the blueprint explicitly names "agent sprawl" as a risk to manage — Bersin's read is that fragmented multi-vendor agent deployments without governance will be the next compliance crisis. Fact: the report launched alongside the (sold-out) Irresistible 2026 conference keynote, and is the analyst frame mainstream HR buyers will encounter at SHRM Annual this week (Signal 03). Inference (labeled): Bersin's frame is the buyer-side mirror of Workday's product-side (Agent Passport, 6/2) and Anthropic's developer-side (Claude / MCP / Stratum's MCP-native posture). All three converge on the same question: how do you know which agents you have, what they do, and that they're safe? Bersin's "agent sprawl" framing names the failure mode that an unverified agent multi-deployment creates; Stratum's published-eval-set posture answers it; Workday's Agent Passport (covered 6/5, 6/8) productizes it. The category-level buyer question is now "show me how each agent is bounded," and the analyst voice is amplifying it.
