01 L4 · Critical Regulatory · EU · Pay transparency · Recruiting

EU Pay Transparency Directive: T-9 days.

2026-06-07 is the binding transposition deadline for Directive (EU) 2023/970. All 27 member states must have national law in place by that date; the European Commission re-confirmed the date as final in May 2026 despite implementation slippage in roughly half the member states [1][2]. Three rules become enforceable the moment a state's transposing law takes effect: (a) candidates must be told the initial salary or salary range before an interview; (b) employers cannot ask about salary history; (c) employees gain a right to information about pay levels for the same or equivalent work [3][4]. The pre-interview disclosure and salary-history ban apply to all employers regardless of size [3]. Annual gender pay-gap reporting starts in 2027 for employers with 250+ employees, against 2026 baseline data [4][8] — so the data-collection clock has already started.

Why this matters to Stratum

Stratum Recruiter is the surface where the recruitment-stage rules bite first. Customers with any EU employees — Cordova has Frankfurt operations; Meridian Energy is German-domiciled — need an answer to "does your ATS surface the salary range to candidates before the interview, and does your eval set treat a salary-history question as a red flag?" by next Friday. Every offer extended in EU jurisdictions from now through year-end becomes 2027-reporting evidence. Stratum's per-decision audit log and people-graph schema can already carry most required fields; what is missing is the customer-facing posture statement and one or two specific schema confirmations.

  • ForgeConfirm the people-graph schema carries: salary-range-at-posting, salary-range-disclosed-to-candidate (boolean + timestamp), salary-history-question-asked (boolean, default false). If any field is missing, scope as a v3 schema patch with Pillar this week.

02 L3 · Action recommended Regulatory · NYC AEDT · Litigation · Compliance

NYC AEDT enforcement gets teeth.

2025-12-02: the NY State Comptroller's audit of NYC DCWP's enforcement of Local Law 144 (the AEDT bias-audit law) declared the 2023–2025 enforcement posture "ineffective" [5][6]. Concrete findings: 75% of 311-line AEDT complaint calls were misrouted and never reached DCWP; DCWP's own review of 32 companies found one violation while the Comptroller's auditors found at least 17 potential violations across the same set; bias-audit reviews were "superficial" and lacked formal procedures [5]. DCWP committed publicly to a "substantially more rigorous" 2026 posture [6]. Penalty math: $500 per violation, $500–$1,500 per day for repeats — and each use of a non-compliant AEDT can constitute a separate violation [5]. Mobley v. Workday remains in the background: the nationwide ADEA collective was certified in February 2026 and discovery is ongoing [7].

Why this matters to Stratum

The U.S. AI-hiring liability surface is hardening at the city level (NYC enforcement) and the federal-court level (Mobley discovery) simultaneously. Buyers in NYC who paused AEDT-compliance investments in 2024–2025 because enforcement was theatre now face a credible 2026 enforcement risk and a public Comptroller report to point at. Stratum's stance — published bias-audit methodology, per-decision audit log, eval-set bias probes — is the right answer to the question NYC employers are about to ask their vendors. Compass should treat this as a sales-enablement window, not just a compliance brief.

Recommended actions

  • CompassUpdate the Stratum AEDT-readiness page to cite the Comptroller audit by date and finding. Pair Stratum's posted bias-audit summary with the Mobley case-law framing. NYC-headquartered prospect outreach gets this as the lead asset for 30 days.
  • ForgeConfirm Stratum's published bias-audit summary meets the DCWP "formal procedures" bar the Comptroller said was missing. If our methodology is more rigorous than DCWP's own checklist, say so on the page with a citation.
  • KernelPromote the Mobley + AEDT combined narrative into the standing eval-set probe set — specifically, age-band disparate-impact monitoring on the candidate-ranking surface. Tie to the bias-audit summary so the eval set and the audit reference the same numbers.
03 L2 · Watch Competitive · MCP · ATS · Distribution

Greenhouse MCP rollout dated to June; third-party wrappers already ship.

Greenhouse's May 7 MCP announcement [9] is now firmly dated as a June 2026 customer rollout — within the next month — exposing what the press release describes as a permission-aware, audit-logged surface across Harvest, Job Board, and Ingestion APIs [9]. Meanwhile, third-party MCP wrappers for the same ATS surfaces are already live: Zapier ships an Ashby MCP and a Greenhouse MCP [10] and CData ships an independent Greenhouse MCP driver. Tool counts in the wild for Greenhouse are now in the 175-range from one third-party packaging [10]. Stratum currently exposes four MCP servers (graph, console, recruiter, connectors).

Why this matters to Stratum

Two distinct trends to separate. First, tool count in MCP is becoming a vanity metric: a 175-tool Greenhouse MCP packaged by Zapier is not the same as 175 governance-grade tools, but buyer-side procurement language will not initially distinguish. Second, governed vs un-governed MCP is the contested word that follows "MCP-native." Greenhouse's June rollout will explicitly emphasize permissioning and audit; Zapier-style wrappers will emphasize breadth. Stratum sits closer to the Greenhouse posture (governed) than to the Zapier posture (breadth-first). Compass and Forge should be ready for the question "your four servers vs Zapier's 9,000 integrations" with a clear governance frame.

Recommended actions

  • ForgeOne-pager: Stratum's four MCP servers compared on (a) per-call permissioning, (b) audit-log granularity, (c) decision-class classification, (d) eval-set coverage. Frame "four governed" against "175 wrapped" in plain language.
  • CompassAdd to the buyer-FAQ a "MCP tool count vs MCP governance" answer. Tool count alone is not a quality measure; cite the Mobley framing — "what does a plaintiff get when they subpoena the audit log?"
  • EglinStanding checkpoint: re-verify Greenhouse MCP rollout date, governance language, and any customer adoption signals on 2026-06-09 (T+3 days after rollout window opens). Update battlecard if posture shifts.
