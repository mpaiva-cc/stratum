01 L3 · Action Regulatory · EU Digital Omnibus on AI · Council formal-adoption vote scheduled 26 June 2026 (today) · Substantive deferral: Annex III HR/employment obligations 2 Aug 2026 → 2 Dec 2027 (16 months) · Two procedural steps remain after Council vote (Presidents' signature + Official Journal publication) before entry into force · Target adoption before 2 August 2026

Council vote scheduled today. The deferral takes its first procedural step.

Per iubenda's "Parliament adopts the AI Omnibus, Council sign-off next" [1], the Sidley Data Matters Privacy Blog "EU Lawmakers Reach Provisional Agreement to Delay Key EU AI Act Obligations" [2], and Gibson Dunn's "EU AI Act Omnibus Agreement — Postponed High-Risk Deadlines and Other Key Changes" [3], the Council of the EU's formal-adoption vote on the Digital Omnibus on AI is scheduled for today, 26 June 2026, with formal publication in the Official Journal of the EU expected following Council adoption. Fact: the procedural sequence is provisional trilogue agreement 7 May 2026; Member-State confirmation in Council 13 May 2026; European Parliament adoption 16 June 2026 (423 in favor, 57 against, 174 abstentions); Council mandate vote scheduled 26 June 2026; signature by Presidents of both institutions; publication in the Official Journal; entry into force before 2 August 2026. Fact: the substantive deferral covers Annex III high-risk AI system obligations (the use-based category that includes recruitment, applicant evaluation, promotion-and-termination decisions, task allocation, and worker monitoring — Stratum's surface exactly) postponed from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027 — 16 months of additional runway. Fact: Annex I product-regulated obligations are postponed from 2 August 2027 to 2 August 2028 (12 months). Fact: the Omnibus also introduces (Council and Parliament both confirmed) a new prohibition on AI systems generating child sexual abuse material or non-consensual intimate content, applicable from 2 December 2026. Inference (labeled): the Council vote landing as scheduled today closes the first of three remaining procedural loops on a deferral the Compact has been tracking since 2 June; if it lands, the file / do-not-file Annex III consultation submission Forge + Helm are deciding Friday EOD ships with the "built to the original calendar — here is what we shipped" framing on a regulatory record where the original calendar has officially moved; if it slips, the file goes with a slightly sharper framing ("the original calendar holds in principle even as the institutions reshuffle the procedure"). The submission ships either way.

02 L2 · Watch Competitive · Ashby (5/7 MCP-lane vendor) · AI Notetaker opt-in recording-consent customization · Dynamic-filter override rules (location-aware) · EU-region opt-in policy w/ opt-out default elsewhere · Application-form opt-in question, profile-stored · Centralized admin view for AI Notetaker access · Compliance signal: GDPR + EU-US Data Privacy Framework certified

The MCP-lane vendor cluster ships consent. Methodology is what we publish; controls are what they ship.

Per Ashby's product-update pages "Capture opt-in recording consent for AI Notetaker to meet stricter privacy requirements" [4] and "AI Notetaker Updates: Improved user management, reporting, and consent customization" [5], Ashby has shipped (within this brief's window per the recent-update flag) opt-in recording-consent customization for AI Notetaker with three substantive components. Fact: dynamic-filter override rules can keep opt-out as the default policy while requiring opt-in consent for candidates located in the EU or other regions with stricter privacy regulations — the filter is location-based, applied automatically. Fact: the application form now includes a new "Interview Recording Consent" question where candidates opt in or opt out at the application step, with the choice stored on the candidate profile for future scheduling workflows. Fact: a centralized admin view lets administrators manage AI Notetaker access organization-wide — who has it enabled, who is added or removed, filterable by user. Fact: Ashby's AI Notetaker is certified to the Data Privacy Framework including the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, the Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, and the UK Extension, and enters DPAs incorporating European Commission standard contractual clauses. Inference (labeled): Ashby is the third MCP-lane vendor in 30 days to ship region-aware AI-compliance controls (after Workable 13 May and Greenhouse's 25 June "structured hiring, not shortcut it" release that included MCP broadly-available 6 July). The MCP-lane vendor cluster is coalescing not just on Stratum's "structured hiring + human-in-the-loop" register (yesterday's brief) but also on the consent-policy + region-policy compliance affordances Stratum has been describing as the post-MCP differentiation surface. The differentiation surface continues to narrow; the eval-set v1.2 publish on Friday is what distinguishes Stratum from "we shipped consent controls and we GDPR-certified" vendor copy.

The verbatim is now quotable. The second filing has not yet landed.

Per the Duane Morris Class Action Defense blog 24 June post [6], HR Executive's 24 June "Judge refuses to dismiss most Workday hiring bias allegations" [7], and HR Dive's "Workday can't shake California AI discrimination claims" [8] (all corroborated yesterday and re-checked today), the verbatim language from Judge Lin's 22 June order is now in the trade-press primary record. Fact (verbatim): Workday "is not subject to derivative liability but instead is directly liable for its 'own engagement in FEHA-regulated activities on the employer's behalf.'" Fact (verbatim): the FEHA California-nexus theory survives because Workday "allegedly designs, develops, and operates its algorithmic screening tools from its California headquarters" and the third amended complaint alleged Workday's tools were "designed, developed, maintained, and controlled from [Workday's] California headquarters" and the "screening, scoring, and rejection" of plaintiffs' applications "originate[d] in and [was] carried out from California." Fact: as of this brief's window-close, no second plaintiffs'-firm filing using the FEHA California-nexus theory against another California-headquartered HR-tech vendor (Lattice / Eightfold / HireVue / Phenom / Greenhouse) has been publicly observed. Fact: Workday has not issued a public statement on Lin's holding, and the EEOC has not filed a follow-on amicus or guidance memo. Inference (labeled): the verbatim "directly liable for its 'own engagement in FEHA-regulated activities'" language is now quotable in Compass's Friday narrative-shift piece without paraphrase risk, which closes the source-confidence caveat Eglin flagged on 6/25. The plaintiffs'-firm second-filing watch holds — the absence is itself the signal, and Eglin will re-elevate to L3 the moment it lands. The DOJ Office of Legal Counsel's 9 June memo (EEOC disparate-impact guidelines "unconstitutional") is the federal counter-current and explains why the EEOC follow-on has not landed; the practical implication is that California-FEHA + state-AEDT remains the operative private-action vector, which is what yesterday's brief said and what today reaffirms.
